Bouncing back from a more serious post, I thought it would be a good idea to spend some time reflecting on what was one of my favorite events for the year, the release of spotify wrapped!! And let me start by airing out my abundant and profound disappointment.

The stolen, bastardized, and overly commercial has met the zenith of its unspectacular failure in a flop(ify) so barren people were praising apple music. This year's wrapped was a drab release that failed to capture any essence of what makes music listening special. It was devoid of any creativity, any character, and any substance.

I am unsure of its validity when it comes to the rumors of Spotify laying off workers and replacing them with AI. However, I’m generally unconcerned about finding out the truth. People's agitation at this year's wrapped is a result of an increasingly worrisome and tiring trend: lazy, stale, and apathetic releases. So while I may not have the solution right now while costs continue to rise, bloated corporate budgets seemingly stifle creativity, and the commercial machine does nothing but soften what could have otherwise been provocative or interesting creations, I can unabashedly tell you that AI is not the solution. 

If you weren’t already convinced of AI’s pernicious potential because of its environmental implications, moral failures, and the plagiarism running amok, I urge you to listen to what the creatives have been saying. Hear their chorus. The future of art and creation will continue to feature a beige and grey backdrop if we reward laziness. Furthermore, creativity will be repressed in the name of saving time or money, often at the expense of other people’s earnest effort and in the process dismantling what truly makes art special. Whatever it may be - know that any art invested in AI is art divested in humanity.

Looking at wrapped today, I can’t help but wonder what happened to the imagination that made wrappeds the somewhat special event that it was. Unfortunately, it appears to be another piece in the pattern of paltry corporate products. Taking a glance at Spotify today we can see the luster they earned slowly being stripped away. A weak algorithm that rarely promotes genuine music discovery, the recent updates littered with unspectacular additions like AI playlists and playlist covers, and a UI more interested in emulating TikTok than supporting creative exploration and artists all illustrate a giant crumbling in front of us. Not to mention a peculiar divestment from albums. The future of music and music streaming is unclear at best and change seems to be arriving rapidly and randomly. Any questions we have can only be answered by time.

On the wrapped product itself, what can I say that already hasn’t been mentioned. The lack of genres was an unexpected but deep leap back, there were no fun facts or worthwhile conversation starters, and ultimately no spice, just unremarkable mush. The only semi “unique” aspect was a mishmash of randomly generated words that Spotify already went “viral” for when people discovered what daylists were. Not only was nothing of substance showcased they presented an entirely redundant and obsolete release that could be replaced with a 30-second stroll to your own  “on repeat” and “daylist.” While those with more heart could do a quick login to one of the 100 third-party services that seek to answer more interesting questions and present them in an exceedingly more compelling manner. It was lifeless, it was dull, and it was exceedingly plain.

That being said, let me actually do a small dive into my 2024 in music.

I have a complicated relationship with my wrapped music mostly because my style of music listening tends to overly represent and favor the catchier earbug songs over the songs that have more of an emotional impact on me. Not that these two categories are always mutually exclusive. The way I describe my day-to-day music listening consumption is that of an itchy style. Every song I listen to scratches some kind of itch, it’s a lot more of a physical, more reactive listening behavior instead of potentially more mindful, thoughtful listening. It takes me some time after truly enjoying the sound of a song before I really begin to chew on a piece. Truthfully, at least on a day-to-day basis, I treat music as an opportunity to indulge in a kind of bodily pleasure rather than a text to digest. That usually takes a certain amount of time and headspace that appears to become progressively more difficult to afford these days.

Despite it all, there are a lot of pros to this listening style. For instance, I’m not particularly bounded by language or genre in the way some others might be. It’s a lot easier for me to find songs of any type from anywhere that capture the essence of delightful sound. Take a stroll through my top 100 songs of 2024 and a pretty solid picture can be painted. A con however is I tend to listen to music in blocks. For any given set of days or weeks I pick on the order of 5-20 songs, maybe an album that I will listen to on repeat. At the gym, while commuting or walking, at work, etc. What this means is that relative to everyone else I’m quick to saturate a song and can sometimes let my music experience become a race for consumption (aka that next scratch) as opposed to respecting its sensitivity. And while - on occasion - I do find the time to actually sit with my music and find songs that resonate with me beyond the noise, I would be remiss to acknowledge the corpses left behind. The music that I sucked dry before having the chance to appreciate its more complex components. However still, not all songs are left to “die” and for me, it is quite easy to compartmentalize memory and music together. I can often unlock the nostalgic feelings of high school, a relationship, or a relevant period by simply scrolling to the pocket of liked songs I was listening to at that time and pressing play.

This is why I tend to be more secretive about my top songs (and occasionally artists) because it makes it a tad bit difficult to capture the esoteric aesthetic of those who have ostensibly a much deeper connection with the music they consume. Those who know me, know how much pride I take in the diverse repertoire of music I listen to. A long car ride with me features a queue not limited by any theme, filled with a diverse mix of energy, genre, and meaning. Yet I do sometimes feel a bit insecure. Every so often I wonder if I am making up for not being a mile deep by being a mile wide. So, while I am proud of my generally eclectic listening behavior and if I personally try not to care for the most part, your music data still gives off some kind of impression of who you are. And who doesn’t want to feel unique and special in the same way Narcissus must have felt when gazing upon his reflection.

For one, I was really hoping drake wouldn’t make the top 5 and after Rina’s flop year (character not music, but a bit music) I was hoping she might be replaced by the likes of Chappell, Omar, or Raveena (who I personally felt I listened to more). Also, this is the first year since 2018 that Frank Ocean was not in my top 5 #notafakefan, so I was very surprised by that. Otherwise, Pinpantherss’ Heaven Knows when triple platinum in my sony XM-4s and Ravyn Lenae might as well own my car and bedroom at this point, I am not surprised by either of their inclusions. This is actually the first year Beyonce was in my top 5 and I think I can mostly attribute that to the alignment of a trip to Houston, the release of Cowboy Carter, and an interest in indulging in my nostalgia if not the occasional basketball edit.

As for my top songs, I honestly don’t have much to say. You broke my heart was just one of those songs that scratched like no other song scratched. I couldn’t resist playing it on those late-night drives blasting it on my speakers when alone in my car. It rarely made the transition to my headphones, but it would be careless to acknowledge the occasional drunk DC train ride where it slipped into my ears in addition to Young Thug’s Oh U Went (also featuring drake smh). For my second song this might be an overzealous and premature statement, I'll likely disagree later, but How Sweet by New Jeans might actually be one of my favorite KPOP SONGS of all time by undeniably one of my favorite KPOP GROUPS of all time. Definitely generational and I will not debate this. And while it seems How Sweet will be lost(ish) in an otherwise elite, if not limited, discography in terms of its popularity I know it’s one of those songs that just really hit me. New jeans when they lock in really do not miss, but I think How Sweet will always have a special place in my heart. Although, I can just about say the same thing for Super Shy. I was in Vietnam when the song released and a DJ at the club I went to played it in their set the same night it dropped. I will never forget that.

Moving towards the songs, Feel Complete is a song that captures the essence of Pinkpantherss and why I love her so much. Her ability to capture the sorrowful essence of a depressed lover and package that into a pop hit makes it a song almost cater made for me. In respect of time and space, I’ll save my monologue of Pinkpantherss for later, but she is truly a genius of our generation with a voice only the Greek sirens could emulate. As a KPOP fan it is my sincere hope that they stop drawing if not occasionally stealing from her influence and just work with her. Although she did have that remix with Le Sserafim on Crazy and is/was an outward fan of NCT. The same feelings can be expressed for Blue, which although its different message still resonates. Haven’t we all experienced a little unrequited love? Ravyn Lenae’s vocals also just inspire something in me and Bird’s Eye was one of my favorite albums of the year. Love Me Not was just the catchiest of the two singles she dropped a few weeks before the album dropped, although I did have one incredibly tearful drive home with this song that always sticks with me. One small final note is, I was texting this one guy who said all my songs were kind of breakup songs and I never noticed or made that connection, especially since - well - I haven’t broken up with anyone this year lmao. But I still thought it might be cool to mention. Although, I’ve purportedly always had an affinity for the more depressed songs enshrouded in poppy pleasant vocals. You can ask a high school ex to confirm that point.

Finally, a quick shout-out to some of my favorite projects from this year that deserve mentioning IMO.

I do want to add a little piece to acknowledge the songs, artists, or albums that won’t make it into the throws of my listening data because of the timing of their release. Without a doubt Magdalena Bay’s “Imaginal Disk” was such a pleasant surprise. I hadn’t always been a fan and would listen to the occasional single, but this was the first project I really became enamoured with. It’s a highly recommend from me and an album full of spectacular noise if that makes sense. This I think can also be said for Raveena’s “Where the Butterflies Go in the Rain,” which is in essence a beautifully pleasant experience.  Kendrick Lamar’s “GNX” and Tyler’s (my 2022 number 2) “Chromakopia” (much like Imaginal disc if we are being honest) are burdened by their late release and without a doubt would have had some songs make the top 20 if time allowed. Artists that I haven’t mentioned yet but deserving of the minuscule spotlight I can shed on them include: Caroline Polachek found a place this year a few songs I rediscovered, a new jeans mashup with Kelela caused me to dive into her discography again, the ever so famous Clairo, and Veeze seemed to pop up a lot. Finally a small call out to KPOP. I am an avid KPOP listener, but KPOP to me is a medium I engage with more as a sum of its parts for entertainment value rather than explicitly its music production. With this in mind, I spend most of my time consuming KPOP content on youtube and twitter where their more visual and non-auditory components are allowed to bloom and thrive. KPOP songs that lack the necessary ear bug quality to migrate from yt or twitter to spotify, tend to be relegated to other platforms. 

This was definitely more of a brain dump kind of blog post, just wanted to share some knee-jerk thoughts. Thank you for reading!!
