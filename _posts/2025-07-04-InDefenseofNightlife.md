---
title: In Defense of Nightlife
author: Matthew
layout: post
---
I’ve personally been drawn to nightlife and all its alluring elements for as long as I can remember. Intrigue built by the young adult movies and their respective soundtracks of the early 2000s which would occasionally find their way onto my TV screen. However, as someone who engages in the night with a certain level of regularity, I still operate with some apprehension when it comes to actually discussing or sharing my experiences in those spaces. Spaces like the club, the bar, and wherever I end up past three or four in the morning. For what I hope to articulate are many differing reasons, I sense (or possibly project) a kind of disdain or suspicion when it comes to discussing nightlife. Not to say nightlife doesn’t have its pernicious margins, but in my experience, the club and the bar -- just as much as the rec center or the park -- is a site of community, vulnerability, and tenderness. I think people are quick to doubt the depth clubbing and drinking, but I’d like to push against that and hopefully illustrate an arena full of complexity and its own kind of beauty, which has undoubtedly enriched the last year of my life living in Mongolia. 

The most significant swathe of critical opinions against nightlife follows the sentiment that clubbing, drinking, and bars are arenas of socio-sensual pleasure that reflect a kind of hedonism lacking in sophistication. That night clubs and bars are places where young people engage in vapid and shallow fun, essentially indulgent escapism through transgressive behavior. Furthermore, there is a perception that nightlife is not an activity that in any substantive ways cultivates growth or meaningfully challenges oneself. It’s for for these reasons that we see the tik toks of “the trip I showed my parents vs the real trip,” entrepreneurs who document their departure from their depressive drinking and nights out to being a successful money maker, or people who have friends they go out with and friends they don’t. In my case, I just find myself often responding with “nothing much” when asked what I did over the weekend. There lies a common denominator here: going out and drinking is an activity that allows for judgment. The idea being that people who lack goals or ambition go out frequently, it's an unproductive use of your time and demonstrates a disinterest in your development. I’ll be honest, I think that’s weak. 

To get the obvious rebuttal out of the way, nightlife is one of the most effective generators of bonding with its own rules and rituals. People can both ignite and kindle relationships by simply going out and drinking with them. In this sense, real, tangible community building takes place and is established through the similar mechanisms of more “accepted” social activities. I’d like to point to some valuable and meaningful segments of nightlife that help illustrate that. For starters, I think going out and drinking is an exercise in authenticity and vulnerability. When someone chooses to drink, chooses to dance, and chooses to let their inhibitions melt away, they are choosing to be more naked and honest versions of themselves. What this means is they are trusting the people they are going out with not to judge them and more importantly to engage with them. I think that is beautiful. It’s affirming to know that your friends and peers have confidence that you can be there to take care of them, and when they may showcase their more “embarrassing” sides they believe you will not hold judgment. Whether it be a broken heart or terrible dance moves, I see value in people breaking down the walls they build to protect themselves. I would argue that this is an incredibly unique aspect of nightlife, at least in its pace and recurrence. I’m even reminded of discussions I share with my sister regarding girl bathroom culture, where sisterhood and compliments are frequent. Nightlife allows for relationships and personalities to enter a more fluid and honest state. Furthermore, as the desire for “third spaces” is continually being articulated, the bar or club can also serve that function of a location where people tap into their community. Because bars and clubs can be a site of taste where like-minded people connect over shared interests in passions like music, joy, flavors, aesthetics, and everything in between. It’s where certain communities like queer folx, artists, creators, deviants, etc etc can actually find eachother. At least they know these places exist. I know it has been a place for me.

For five months, and now just on occasion, I worked in a lounge/bar/sometimes nightclub as a second job. That adventure has dramatically shaped my experience (positively) living here in Ulaanbaatar. Up until I started working, it had been difficult to build genuine relationships. I had found some success in my interests making solid - if not occasionally superficial - relationships while at the gym or playing basketball, at work, or in various hobbies I tried to tap into. But working at this place, I have made some genuinely worthwhile connections and memories. I’ve become friends with my coworkers like DJs, bartenders, security guards, and other staff, but also been able to meet people in business, travelers, and those from all over the world. Most importantly, within the circle that surrounds my work, both physically and in a more metaphorical sense, I’ve discovered some sense of a community. I’ve found people to share late nights and early mornings with, people who are happy to see me, people who invite me out/share their world with me (and vice versa), and felt a real tenderness in my experience. I’ve made memories with people who I’d like to call my friends that I will cherish for a long time. I’ve learned their stories and what they care about. These are individuals with intense depth and multidimensional personalities. These are the people that ultimately made me feel welcome in a city over 10,000 kilometers away from home. I’m not trying to say this has to be everyone’s community, but nightlife has been some of mine. It can also be anyone's. The club is not simply a hollow room full of drunkards, there is a lot to be learned from really inspiring people.

In this sense, the idea that nightlife is intrinsically shallow is so far from the actual reality. Building on the opportunity for community and relationships, nightlife is full of alternative systems of social knowledge that deserve just as much credit as knowing the best restaurants or nicest cafes, local history, or life hacks. Because knowing where to go, what to listen to, the best drinks, or how to dress, among many, many other things is valuable knowledge too. You can learn a city and its heartbeat in completely different and equally important ways by knowing where the people like to go at night. Think of the bartender who has to understand cocktails with just as much insight as a chef knows their own recipes and is tasked with grasping the complex hermeneutics regarding their customers, all the while continually monitoring the current cultural zeitgeist. What about the DJ who is never just a playlist maker, but rather an artist who is either sculpting and reshaping music or outright creating new and innovative sounds, has to respond to the shifting oceanic emotions of the dancefloor, and constantly evolving so as not to let their sets become redundant. Finally, even the club goer has depth and perception as someone who builds the knowledge of the night, knowing what people go where (and at what time), the vibes and culture of each club or bar, the flavors of their favorite bartenders, and builds connections with the right people. All of these people interact together in an ecosystem that builds upon itself. Learning and growth is continually taking place which has resulted in the development of a new language of meaning within every individual city. All of this is knowledge and skills that I am constantly impressed by. I’d hope others see this too.

In the end, nightlife doesn’t have to be your thing, but it is for some. I believe it is the narratives often cited against nightlife that are reductive and shallow. Furthermore, they are a slippery slope that can perpetuate destructive and harmful ideas. Concepts like nightlife host the sites that are bad and where bad things happen are intertwined with concepts of misogyny and homophobia. Also, just in case it had to be said, not every activity has to be productive. Beautiful things happen at night. People fall in love, friends get closer, and there is constant learning and growing. The unique kind of ephemeral spontaneity that is just difficult to create or find elsewhere is what allows for such powerful passion and intimacy. Juxtaposed against the banality thrust upon everyone by archaic popular aesthetics of quiet luxury and business casual attire or conservatism that values limiting individuality and expressions serves to prove my point. I love that there is so much individuality whenever I take the step outside into the night. I’ve made many friends and found many people who affirm my presence while choosing to go out and drink a little. Just as we can find beauty in the mundane, we can find complexity and depth in the night. Not everything has to be a conventional intellectual exercise or struggle for it to be worthwhile. Beauty does not always have to be earned. So when I go out at night and I find myself surrounded by meaning, I can't help but feel like I need to defend it just a little bit.
