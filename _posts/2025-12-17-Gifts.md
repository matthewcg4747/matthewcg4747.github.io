Amidst the backdrop of the holidays, I was recently asked to describe the best gift I’ve ever received. Instead of focusing on the grandest or most expensive gifts, I interpreted the question as more so asking which gift resonated with me most on a personal level.The way I would think most people would approach the question. And while it may not be the “best” gift in any conventional sense, the first memory that rushed to my mind was a rather inconsequential Christmas gift I received as a freshman in college.

On a fall afternoon in 2019 a close friend and I were wandering through one of the malls nearby our campus. When you live somewhere like Claremont, most of your time off campus is spent in unassuming places like these, halfhearted attempts to quell the boredom that defines the suburban sprawl of Southern California. This afternoon, we happened to wander into a PacSun featuring a Dr. Seuss collaboration amidst the vestiges of nostalgic partnerships that dominated fast fashion during the late 2010s (Think Rugrats, Simpsons, Spongebob, etc). Upon noticing the many options I made a passing comment about wanting something Dr. Seuss themed, a “victim” to the sentimentality these companies were hoping to prey on. Regardless, as quickly as I had muttered the remark, the thought had left my mind. There were other clothes to have opinions on. At the time it was simply a reaction, nothing more.

It was just a few months later, when this same friend and I exchanged Christmas gifts before the holiday break, that I unwrapped an incredibly pleasant surprise. Tearing apart the paper I discovered a shirt Dr. Seuss' art framed within the outline of the Cat in the Hat and one of his books containing a written note inside the front cover. Almost instantly I understood the meaning and was flooded with a collision of emotions like confusion, admiration, even guilt, but above all, I was a bit shocked. The gift was unmistakably intentional. We had only been friends for a few months, when we were in the mall even less, and the comment made in PacSun was fleeting at best. Yet as if it was proof of our afternoon together here on my lap lay a shirt and a book.

In isolation, the gift appears rather ordinary. But to an 18 year old living 2,500 miles away from home, it represented something much deeper. It was the first time since leaving, possibly one of the earliest times I truly felt this way too, that someone was communicating to me that I was worth remembering. Because to be loved, is to be remembered.

I’ve come to believe that memory is partly a choice. In love and intimacy, you can say the right things, you can move with consistency and patience, or even buy the “right” gifts. Yet, there is a distinct contrast between loving someone and making them feel loved, and, for me, in memory lies the difference-maker. Grand gestures may be impressive, possibly even conveying a significant magnitude of sacrifice, but they are singular. I’d like you to consider what remembering signifies. What matters more is the proof that your care does not fade with time, but deepens. Will you choose to remember the people you cherish and honor their presence in your life?

While this season I find myself thinking about memory in the context of gift giving, this mindset applies broadly to relationships. Intimacy is a form of literacy and at its core is legibility. The question just becomes, can you truly see someone? Just as you might remember their allergies or taste as you prepare a meal, when it comes to gifts, are you paying attention to their offhand remarks, the way they interact with their surroundings, the details of their stories and memories, or the small preferences they reveal without realizing it? Gift giving emerges as a test of fluency, how well do you understand the people you love, and can you translate that understanding into something tangible yet full of emotional energy.

This holiday season, I’m excited to flex my emotional memory. I want to look back on our shared moments and show the people I care about how worth remembering they are. I may not be able to give gifts that dazzle with spectacle and magnitude, but I will put together gifts with a special kind of emotional weight. Gifts that were shaped by countless conversations and mutual experiences, because the small details that I choose to compartmentalize are where love transcends.

As I think of my friends and loved ones this approaching Christmas and New Years I am often full of different ideas. Perhaps for my friend who likes to eat, it’ll be a basket of snacks we have shared or a nod to the nostalgic goods you point out when we pass through the grocery store. Maybe it’s the tools for that hobby you wanted to try, the clothes you wanted but couldn’t quite afford at the time, or the fragrance we once gushed over. If possible, I’ll get you gifts that reflect our experiences, a CD from the artist we waited 12 hours to see, posters from our movie nights at home, or a collage full of our adventures. I want my gifts to reflect the quiet, yet sharp beauty of being remembered. In being remembered, I hope to illustrate the indelible mark on my heart and soul that the people I love have made.

In no way is anything I am saying intended to be black and white. Relationships are complex, composed of many elements, and are rarely dependent on a singular foundation. However, this way of thinking is a guiding principle of my life. It appears in the letters I write, the ways I show up for others, and, before 2026 formally arrives, it will inform the gifts I intend to give. Some of the best parts of love live in remembering. Not in impulse or intensity, but rather in something cooler and steadier. The memories that surface while lying awake in bed, reappear when you pass the places that used to be yours, or in the parts that shape their whole.

I like to think of memory much like the gold that joins the broken pieces in Kintsugi. While not predicated on the existence of fractured parts, I do like the idea of something beautiful, lustrous binding the whole together. In the same sense memory is the shine that connects the mundane to the beautiful. It’s why the gifts I give must carry a story, a reason, a memory, because there is something so profoundly beautiful about being remembered. I’m reminded of the scarf from a friend who remembered my new home was the coldest capital city in the world and the playlist they made for my 3:00 am walks home, the basketball I needed for the new school year, and of course, the Dr. Seuss shirt from a fall afternoon in California. These gifts evolved when the material was joined to the metaphysical, and as such. I still carry those gifts with me. It’s my deepest hope that with each gift I can make the people in my life feel the same way, proof of their impact and charm.
