As is the case with every fall, the proverbial “cuffing season” has arrived and I wanted to take this opportunity to reflect on relationships, love, and intimacy. I think I want to start by addressing the crisis of sincerity, a consequence of desire’s perception as humiliation. Or, more colloquially, the “Nonchalance Epidemic.”

As many, more qualified and more articulate social observers have addressed before me, there has been a distinct shift away from earnest desire and enthusiasm toward the performance of indifference, or rather the protection of indifference. There is a prevailing sentiment that appearing too excited, too interested is embarrassing and borderlines on some kind of humiliation ritual. It’s a product of social media scrutiny and a culture that insists on mocking caring about anything. I’d even argue this attitude reaches as far back as the early 2000s with the rise of media like South Park and a zeitgeist that departed from the heartfelt sincerity of silk shirt R&B. The product is a culture that conditions individuals to become hyper-curated representations of themselves, defined by a veneer of ease and emotional distance. The pursuit of the most esoteric version of ourselves has led to a hollow way of living. We might be more polished and protected from shame, but the price of security has been authenticity and intimacy. So while we are liked, there will always be that lingering question of whether or not we are truly loved. This pursuit of a false invulnerability, of never being hurt, judged, or put down, has inevitably bled into every facet of our lives: in how we create, in the futures we imagine, how we interact with our environment and the people around us, and, crucially, how we love.

In 2025 it’s interesting, if not disappointing, to see how so much of what made romance so sharp and special has been replaced with the calculated strategy of (artificial) scarcity. We currently inhabit a manufactured world where affection and desire function more like currency; everyone wants to be the person left holding all their cards, or rather, the leverage. Vulnerability has become a liability. Instead of professing our interest openly, we walk a tight rope of half-measures, offering morsels of intimacy. Fragments that we hope will convince someone that we are enough. God forbid we seem too eager. If you’ll allow me a quick analogy, I’d liken this cultural shift in love to the fight choreography of 1960s-1990s kung fu cinema juxtaposed to Hollywood action films..

Early martial arts movies, particularly those from China and Hong Kong, embraced a freer flowing, spontaneous, and artistic approach to their fight scenes. Many of their most famous action stars were genuine martial arts masters, and rather than following strict choreography, they improvised, building on each other’s energy to create something fluid and alive. These fights moved like a dance facilitated through a chemistry of spectacle. By contrast, Hollywood opted for what I would describe as meticulous, rigid choreography. Instead of a dynamic dance, American action films created sequences where every step, every punch, and every interaction was pre-planned. Furthermore, while kung fu movies enjoyed long singular takes that welcomed inefficiency as a means to facilitate tighter immersion, engagement, and authenticity. Hollywood prioritized polish with an overindulgent use of editing, jump cuts, and close-ups that favored a brutal efficiency in the name of maximizing impact.

I bring this up because I think modern relationships have unfortunately followed Hollywood’s lead. What was once an alluring and improvisational dance has become a set of rehearsed steps that can be best described as a choreography of connection. Trying has become “cringe.” Wanting something earnestly feels embarrassing, almost as though you are breaking some taboo. And so, we chase an artificial polish, facilitating environments where sincerity is dampened and where we create multiple exits from commitment. Molding ourselves and actions after what we know works, because everyone deep down wants to be seen, adored, and most importantly, chosen. Which I say as someone who has taken solace in being safe, in a position of protection and comfort, despite operating in fear. But, I think there’s a cure, one that’s difficult and not easily administered, but without a doubt vital to ameliorating the predicament we find ourselves in: to love loudly.

The best way out of the fog of callousness is going to be right through the very vulnerability many of us have been socialized to fear. Allow love and passion to flow without restraint, to care deeply, completely, and without fear - tearing down the walls we have erected to shield us from pain. Because yes, to welcome love does mean to welcome pain, and yes, even sometimes cringe. Even if love can lack elegance, be awkward or confusing, and even unexplainable or illogical, love also transforms. It changes how you think, how you see, how you feel, and the way you act. Love turns imagined futures into reality and transforms life into art. So while opening yourself to love opens you to hurt and loss, it also invites the extraordinary.

At the fulcrum of authenticity lies honesty. Frankly, I’m tired of performing detachment.  I’ve exhausted myself pretending to be someone who doesn't care (#realyearner, iykyk), hiding pieces from the people I love and the people I hope to grow closer with. In manufacturing an artificial ceiling, I have left those relationships hollow and fragile. In carving myself into a figure for others' desires, I’ve become fluent in other people’s preferences. Not my own. I’m resolved to be the most honest and whole version of myself, even if that is a person who loves clumsily, falters, or isn’t perfect for everyone. As much as it will hurt to hear sometimes. I welcome vulnerability despite the pain it can bring, because it opens doors that only raw authenticity can. Just picture the most nail-biting scenes from Wong Kar-Wai’s early films. I know I’ll hurt, I know I will cringe at what I say or do. But even our low moments have value, because every moment (beautiful or messy) will be stepping stones towards a more full, confident, and complex human being. 

The lesson is as old as time: it is better to have loved and lost than to have never loved at all. We have a distinctly short time on this earth, a flicker so quick at a cosmic scale, why would I want to waste it in the pursuit of protection? When I leave this Earth I want to be proud of the life I lived, and this includes not leaving anything on the table. So when I say that humanity's greatest strength is our willingness to try and our capacity to care, I mean it. I plan to love wholly moving forward, to shape a future where people love fully and authentically, where they celebrate love because it makes their hearts beat and after it all, they are successful in love because they choose to walk their path, not in spite of a hyper-cautious lifestyle. People were made to care. And to make it abundantly clear. I care. Or, at least I’m willing to try.

While this essay has largely focused on love in the context of relationships (which includes the platonic ones), I also wanted to take a little bit of time to say this same spirit should extend to other corners of your life.. Really, to life itself. Please find it in yourself to love life and the world all around us. Because, as corny as Jordan Peterson is, he really ate when he said, “There are cathedrals everywhere for those with the eyes to see.” If you choose to love life, your life will become a life worth living. 

To this extent, a life worth living doesn’t need to be loud or cinematic; if you fall in love with the ordinary and find beauty in the mundane, you’ll quickly find that life is full of romance. Fall in love with the sounds of the city when you walk home, proof that life is around you despite how alone you might feel. A reminder of the entire worlds, no universes that are passing you by. Just as much as you might value the quiet and serene nooks around you that feel like a secret between you and the city. Find beauty in the effort and generosity of others who decide to care despite their own struggles, who choose to pick up the fallen scooter, feed the stray dogs, or lift those who have fallen. Celebrate the music that moves you, the movies that ignite something in your stomach, the books that comfort you, the clothes that bring you confidence, and any and everything that brings you joy. Separate them from their cultural acclaim, whether or not they are submissive, or whatever they might signal. Discover meaning in the aches, pains, and mistakes, proof of a life survived, fingerprints of a life worth living.

Life and love are never going to be about having the perfect one. The quest for perfection will be perpetual and never-ending. The magic of life and love lies in choice. Choose to live. Choose to love. When you do, every part of your life can begin to feel like one worth cherishing.
