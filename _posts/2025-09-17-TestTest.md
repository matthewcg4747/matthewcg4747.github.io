You never know what memories will can come back to you and when they will. The moments usually relegated to the desert of the benign, barron, and forgotten can light up for even the briefest of moments. Those are my favorites. I do my very best to hold on to those.

When people ask me about my time abroad and I’m forced to reflect, reckon with my time away from family, friends, and loved ones immersed in the supposedly “transformative” juncture, the moments that hold the most affect (i.e. emotional reosnance or energy) are never the sharpest or conventionally “memorable” moments. While I certainly cherish the nights (mornings?) leaving the karoake room at nine in the morning or the precarious sides of working at a nightclub/lounge, they certainly make for the best stories, I can’t help but feel they occasionally reflect a kind of narcissistic hedonism and just as equally, a necessary disregard. In all honesty, what sticks with me are the intimate, otherwise mundane moments. The kinds of little points that hold meaning to no one else but me.

I recently took a two week trip to Korea admist a confusing period for myself and I had a few moments that still linger with me. The one I want to focus on, the initial flicker, was the result of a 3D display at the Seoul (Namsan) tower. The exhibit sparked the chain of memories taking me back to one of the earliest memories I have from a simple afternoon with my dad.
