I’ve recently been working on some applications for grad school and, like many people in this world, trying to get a firmer grip on who I really am. While discovering aspects of my personality, character, and preferences can flow quite seamlessly when given time and interaction, I am often the type to struggle when asked to express myself quickly, bluntly, and with instant depth. The most apt description (analogy?) I can offer up is that getting to know me is much more of a meticulous process, like that of creating or observing a tapestry. Each fiber, each line, each piece is ultimately telling you something, because every part of it necessitates intention. Each tapestry is a creation that requires scrutiny and, with time, understanding. However, in a sense, I lack flair. I don’t immediately tell you something like a sculpture or the infamous abstract pieces of the 2010s. So, what I might not have in loud and ostentatious flare, I might make up in answers and detail. A kind of aura that I’d like to label as discoverable through experience in esoteric observation.

In hopes of feeling more certain of the foundation I am standing on, I wanted to try an exercise in self-reflection. Specifically, one where I attempt to articulate 20 different things?? Pieces of myself, fun facts, feelings, desires, dreams, whatever might come to my mind while sitting and writing them down. Some may be commonly known, some more obscure, some benign, some hopefully interesting, and most of them exceedingly insignificant and incoherent. Regardless, when this essay goes up, I know they will be solid pieces of myself that I offer up to “eternity.” 

So welcome to a list of 20 scattered and random "parts" of me.
 
1. I hate sitting in front of people. I much prefer sitting next to someone when hanging out, at a restaurant, or, truthfully, anywhere other than someone’s home. In my opinion, it relieves all the pressure brought about by eye contact, opens up more spontaneity through observation, and allows conversation to flow more freely. In a way, I think it also makes silence easier to bear since you aren’t so transfixed on the person sitting in front of you and how heavy the silence looks. Tangentially, I love people watching and I think sitting on a bench with someone in a park or, especially, walking together while talking is by far the superior way to have conversations.
2. I really value observation and memory. People who are able to pick up on what others say and recall that later, notice how people move in a room, and who make an effort to reference or call on those memories, no matter how insignificant or superfluous they might be is PEAK. Since I value memory and observation so much it is often one of my “strongest” (?) love languages. I will (try to) cling to every conversation we have, store every small comment you muttered while walking through stores, every fun fact, affinities you might have, etc etc, so that I may reference them in letters, in my own acts of service, and of course in gifts. Everyone probably does this in some capacity, but for me, I want it to be very clear that my actions carry the heavy weight of intention and reason. I do this, I got this, I remembered this, I thought of this because YOU are a person who I choose to cherish in my memories and translate into reason. In the same vein, I can find forgetful people frustrating, particularly when it comes time for them to deliver on what they said.
3. If I had to pick a Letterboxd top four, I would say they are, in no particular order, Fantastic Mr. Fox, Chungking Express, maybe Castle in the Sky (pending rewatch, possible Porco Rosso), and, I don’t know, Batman (?). I tend to be drawn to movies that do a great job of depicting raw and honest life. Perspective enhancing movies so to speak. Think, movies like The Florida Project, Perfect Days, the aforementioned Chungking Express, etc. I think I generally get the most engrossed in movies where the emotions are primary. Not that I am unconcerned with plot or message, rather the movies that stick with me the longest allow the emotions of its characters and story to flow in abundance. I also want to take this opportunity to share that the skill that impresses me the most among storytellers is when they have the ability to take a character that I have grown to genuinely dislike and flip my perception to one of genuine pleasure. Not empathy, nor understanding. That can be done easily, but while betrayal is easy, genuine redemption is hard to successfully accomplish. Oh, and my favorite anime is Cowboy Bebop, over the last year my favorite read was probably “Notes of a Crocodile” by Qiu Miaojin, and the first time I cried for a video game was Telltale’s Walking Dead.
4. I don’t much care for photography that makes life look like a movie. If I want to watch a movie, then I will watch a movie; I don’t need my photography to depict fantasy, surrealism, or abstraction. I prefer photography that provides snapshots with a genuine perspective. That is not me saying I don’t appreciate those who play with shadows, lighting, framing, etc. What I’m really trying to say is that I genuinely value photography that captures life (obvious trend here) and the world. It’s why I love going through people’s camera rolls, to look through the photos on their walls, and see what they choose to capture. For me it’s not really about finding the best photographs as much as I want to see YOUR photographs. If I could offer up a quick comparison, the fast novelty of a photo that captures and frames the full aesthetic of the room in a cool café is less compelling to me than the awkward, potentially loose photo of the weathered cup that caught your eye. Technique is important, but not at the cost of perspective. How do you see the world we share differently, what do you choose to notice that I may not consider or see, and what do you decide to or not to capture? There just really is an art in simply noticing, and that is an art I find particularly beautiful and moving.
5. I’m quite easy to please, but difficult to impress. At least as it pertains to consumption.
6. I’m obsessed with moving water. Rivers, streams, creaks, waves, and my most preferred, waterfalls. These are often my favorite places to relax and visit when traveling. Outside of an innate fascination, if I had to give it a reason, it would likely be enjoying where water and sound intersect. In a way, the noise of the water kills all the noise in my head, so I often can find myself relaxing by moving water for hours without worry or stress. It calms me.
7. I am often one who struggles to just DO. Therefore, I am often genuinely impressed whenever I meet people who just create and make anything. I am never one who really values perfection. Instead, as I think I have made abundantly clear with my other bullet points, I tend to value perspective much more. I was always struck by Vince Staples’ view of art as translation. “You’re translating your emotions, your environment, your point of view, your perspective to outside people.” Individuals have the opportunity to convey whatever they want, but doing that effectively can be very difficult, and this is especially true if you are someone who tries to use your art in a very specific or intentional manner. So, I have a great deal of respect for those who create, because I find it very difficult and arduous. This informs my approach to the consumption of art with respect and tenderness, because I value the fact that someone out there is trying to say something.
8. My favorite color is purple, particularly shades of lavender.
9. I genuinely like it when others are taken care of before me. I like to be the last to eat once everyone has their fill, to be at the back of the group walking while everyone else talks, to be the last one home after everyone gets back safe, to carry the heaviest things back inside, to get the most banged up textbook, driving the car during the road trip, in the worst place to sleep, and of course walking to get the door first (among many other things). Taking care of others brings me genuine joy, and for some, possibly weird (?), reason, I sometimes take pride if it comes at my expense or labor.
10. As for things I like doing with people. I enjoy taking naps in the same room as other people that are awake and talking, I like to experience mornings with my friends where we are all didn’t get enough sleep but don’t have to rush, I like to talk to people while walking (be it on the phone or in person), I think movies are best shared in a group or with friends, I love to play five-on-five basketball where everyone on my team are my friends from high school, I enjoy late nights out and pushing the envelope of fatigue, I really enjoy watching music videos together, I really actually enjoy hosting people at my house, doing festive activities for the holidays, participating in any form of competitive tournaments, and of course a little gossip here and there. These are my plain enjoyments, but still mine.
11. I would say most of my days possess a fog of melancholy that disperses and returns at extremely random and inconsistent times. I just know that at least once every day I can expect to feel some kind of uncertain pensiveness of which I often have no immediate or obvious source to point to. Weirdly, I’ve grown to kind of appreciate these moments because they are some of the only occasions I feel genuinely present and grounded in reality.
12. Above all, I value kindness, generosity, and a tender heart. No matter what someone might have in terms of intrigue, talent, spunk, creativity, voice, and everything in between, the way they interact with the world around them must center on feelings of empathy. Furthermore, I’d like to emphasize tenderness. I think many of us walk paths, live lives, and at least witness a great deal of pain frequently. It's easy to let this pain mold us into avatars of indifference. I think it takes a great deal of courage and strength to see that pain, to experience it, and despite every reason to be callous, instead march forward with kindness in your heart. Not to judge, not to decide, but to move with patience, grace, and understanding. To be tender is to humanize in a world that strips people of their complexity. I don’t blame people for their faults or their anger; I just have a great deal of appreciation for those who rise above it.
13. On that note, I cherish inefficiency, friction, and imperfection. There is this post-WWII and Silicon Valley American obsession with flattening everything. To create a world where we can presumably have access to anything and everything without any crunch. But a life without conflict is a life full of complacency. Imperfection is what gives art personality, mistakes are what give creations character and history, and friction is what makes it possible to live a rewarding life. It’s not that I want problems, it’s that I believe a life without effort isn’t really a life worth living. AKA occasionally, discomfort is the price for a fulfilling life and multidimensional relationships.
14. I would describe myself as a sentimentalholic and nostalgiaholic who hordes anything that might hold any level of affect (aka “an orienting force rooted in emotion” - u/Fishing-Bear). This includes two shoe boxes full of (mostly) every letter I’ve ever received (even from those who have probably forgotten who I am), every little knick-knack or small kitschy gift I can hold on to, souvenirs, and random momentous that I can fit into them. Trust that no matter how ephemeral, brief, or short our time together was, if you have given me something to remember you by it will make itself into the shoebox eventually. Or at a minimum, I’ll hold on to it for as long as I reasonably can.
15. When I was young my dream was to play professional basketball in the NBA. I quickly realized that was not going to happen, but a kid can still dream.
16. I would say that my Chameleon badges go crazy. For those who don’t understand, that means I have the ability to kind of change, shift, and mirror my personality according to who I am with. It can certainly be disorienting on occasion when you question whether or not you genuinely possess some underlying personality, nor would I say that I am always picking the best “camouflage,” and I wouldn’t exactly classify it as code switching. However, what it does mean is that I have made it so that everyone knows a slightly different version of myself.
17. If I really could pick a niche talent, I guess I would say that if you were to give me any album to listen to I could very quickly identify which song(s) either has or will most likely have the most streams. I haven’t actually tested this talent, but the friends I discuss music with could vouch for my tendency to quickly identify the songs that will become more popular or viral.
18. I catch crushes like candy. I don’t fall hard, but I do fall often. Just as the radio version says, “I’m not a player, I just crush a lot.” I also tend to manufacture feelings or meaning in my mind when it wanders and when I am alone. I love to think that other people think and speak of me. That I have this whole entire persona or life outside of the one that I am literally living and instead one that travels through people’s words, perceptions, and thoughts. AKA despite a dearth in deep crushes, I would still categorize myself as a romantic and, of course, a yearner. I wonder if they are thinking about me now….
19. I don’t think most people know this other than my family, I have consumed a great deal of musical theater in my life and would consider myself an avid fan of the performance art.
20. I genuinely enjoy writing letters for other people. The activation energy for doing it can be really hard to muster most times, but it is by far my favorite way to express intimacy with the people I care about in my life. This even extends to people whom I am also not close with, because it can be so fun to see their reaction when you talk about or include information they didn’t expect. I mean, just about everyone enjoys receiving a card. Just as much, and as I have kind of referenced in some of the other facts, receiving written gifts is also my favorite gift to receive. If I could articulate it for myself, cards are not natural. Life is full of souvenirs and mementos, the cups from the job you shared, the merch from your school, the medal from your team, the toy from the restaurant, etc etc, that gradually build up and enter your life. So physical gifts rarely carry the same weight. They certainly can when you are able to connect them to memories or meaning, but prose is special. You have to decide to sit down, think, and reflect. It's almost an obtuse decision to write for someone else because you can't just walk into or discover a letter. Furthermore, prose will always be mine and mine alone, because a letter will forever and can only ever have a singular audience: me.

If you made it this far, thank you for reading. The essays I drafted before felt like they had some heavyish lifting so I opted for one that is a little less formal this time around. Maybe I’m less interesting than I thought. I’m sure over the coming days I’ll hammer my head over forgetting to put in this or that, whatever I imagine as being actually more compelling than what I shared today. Regardless, it is my hope that you hopefully learned something about me, maybe sparked some thought of yourself, or at a minimum enjoyed hearing a bit more about my perspective.
